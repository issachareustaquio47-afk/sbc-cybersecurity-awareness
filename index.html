KNOW THE RISKS

Common threats students face

Cyber threats can appear in school emails, social media, gaming platforms, messages, and even group chats.

🎣

Phishing

Fake messages or websites designed to trick you into giving away passwords or personal information.

WATCH FORUrgent requests, suspicious links, fake login pages.
🔑

Weak Passwords

Short, reused, or predictable passwords make multiple accounts vulnerable at once.

WATCH FORSame password everywhere, names, birthdays, simple patterns.
👤

Social Engineering

Attackers manipulate people into revealing information or doing something unsafe.

WATCH FORImpersonation, pressure, fake authority, emotional manipulation.
📱

Unsafe Apps & Files

Unknown apps, attachments, and downloads can contain harmful software or steal information.

WATCH FORUnknown senders, cracked software, suspicious downloads.
RESPECT ONLINE

Cyberbullying & Online Harassment

Cyberbullying is a serious threat to student well-being. Learn how to recognize, respond, and report it.

💬

What is Cyberbullying?

Using digital platforms to harass, threaten, embarrass, or target another person repeatedly.

EXAMPLESMean comments, spreading rumors, fake accounts, sharing private photos.
🛑

How to Respond

Do not reply or retaliate. Save evidence, block the person, and report the behavior.

REMEMBERYou are not alone. Tell a trusted adult or school counselor.
🤝

Be an Upstander

Support classmates who are being bullied. Report abuse instead of liking or sharing it.

ACTIONSpeak up, offer support, and promote kindness online.
📞

Where to Get Help

Contact your school guidance office, a parent/guardian, or a trusted teacher immediately.

REPORTSchool counselors, barangay officials, or local authorities.

MALICIOUS SOFTWARE

Malware, Ransomware & Viruses

Malicious software can infect your devices, steal your data, or lock your files until you pay. Learn how to stay protected.

🦠

Malware

Software designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to your device.

PREVENTInstall antivirus, avoid cracked software, scan downloads.
🔐

Ransomware

Locks your files and demands payment to restore access. Often spreads through phishing emails.

PREVENTBack up files regularly, never open suspicious attachments.
👾

Trojan Horses

Disguised as legitimate software but secretly installs malicious programs in the background.

PREVENTDownload only from official app stores and trusted websites.
📡

Spyware

Secretly monitors your activity, collects passwords, and tracks your browsing habits.

PREVENTUse anti-spyware tools, avoid suspicious browser extensions.
SURF SMART

Safe Browsing Scenarios

Practice makes prepared. Review these three real-life scenarios to learn how to browse safely online.

📧

Scenario 1: Suspicious Email

Situation: You receive an email claiming to be from your school's registrar, saying your enrollment will be cancelled unless you click a link and log in immediately.

Safe Action: Do not click the link. Open a new browser tab, go to your school's official website directly, and check your enrollment status. Contact the registrar through a known phone number or official email to verify.

LESSONUrgency is a red flag. Always verify through a trusted, independent channel.
🛒

Scenario 2: Online Shopping Deal

Situation: While browsing social media, you see an ad for a 90% discount on a popular gadget. The site asks for your card details and looks slightly different from the official brand.

Safe Action: Check the URL carefully for misspellings (e.g., "sh0pee.com" instead of "shopee.ph"). Look for the padlock icon and "https://". If unsure, go to the official app or website and search for the product there.

LESSONIf a deal seems too good to be true, it probably is. Verify the website before entering any payment details.
📶

Scenario 3: Public Wi-Fi

Situation: You are at a café or mall and connect to free public Wi-Fi. You then log into your school email and online banking account.

Safe Action: Avoid logging into sensitive accounts on public Wi-Fi. If you must, use a VPN (Virtual Private Network) or your mobile data instead. Turn off file sharing and "automatically connect to open networks" in your device settings.

LESSONPublic Wi-Fi is not secure. Anyone on the same network may be able to intercept your data.
EXTRA LAYER OF SECURITY

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) Explained

2FA adds a second step when you log in, so even if someone steals your password, they still cannot access your account.

📱

Authenticator Apps

Apps like Google Authenticator or Authy generate a 6-digit code that changes every 30 seconds. This is the most secure and recommended type of 2FA.

BESTWorks even without internet or cell signal.
✉️

Email or SMS Codes

A one-time code is sent to your email or phone number. Easy to set up, but SMS can be intercepted by SIM-swap attacks.

GOODBetter than no 2FA at all.
🔑

Hardware Security Keys

Physical USB or NFC keys (like YubiKey) that you plug in or tap to log in. Extremely secure and phishing-resistant.

STRONGESTBest for high-value accounts like email and banking.
✅

Where to Enable 2FA

Turn on 2FA for your school email, Google account, Facebook, Instagram, and any banking or shopping app.

PRIORITYStart with your most important accounts first.
PHISHING VARIATIONS

Different Types of Phishing Attacks

Phishing comes in many forms. Knowing the different types helps you recognize them faster.

📧

Email Phishing

Mass emails pretending to be from a bank, school, or popular service. They often have generic greetings and suspicious links.

COMMONCheck the sender's real email address, not just the display name.
🎯

Spear Phishing

Targeted attacks aimed at a specific person, using personal details to seem more convincing. Common against students and teachers.

DANGEROUSBe cautious if a message knows too much about you.
📲

Smishing (SMS Phishing)

Fake text messages claiming you won a prize, have a package, or need to verify your account. Links often lead to fake login pages.

TEXTNever click links in unexpected text messages.
🎙️

Vishing (Voice Phishing)

Phone calls from scammers pretending to be from your bank, school, or government. They pressure you to give personal information.

CALLHang up and call the official number yourself.
GAME SAFE

Staying Safe While Gaming Online

Online games are fun, but they are also targets for scammers and hackers. Protect your account and your personal information.

01

Never Share Account Details

No legitimate game company will ask for your password. Beware of "free skins" or "free diamonds" scams.

02

Use a Unique Password

Do not reuse your school or email password for gaming accounts. Use a separate, strong password.

03

Enable 2FA on Game Accounts

Protect your progress, purchases, and personal data with two-factor authentication.

04

Don't Trust Strangers

Scammers pose as fellow players. Never send money, gift cards, or personal info to someone you met in-game.

05

Download Games from Official Stores

Cracked or modded games often contain malware. Only use Google Play, App Store, Steam, or official sites.

06

Keep Voice Chat Safe

Do not reveal your real name, school, address, or age to strangers in voice or text chat.

BUILD GOOD HABITS

Your 6-step safety checklist

Small habits make a big difference. Use this checklist before you go online.

01

Use strong, unique passwords

Use a different password for important accounts. A password manager can help.

02

Turn on two-factor authentication

2FA adds another layer of protection even if someone learns your password.

03

Think before clicking

Check the sender, URL, spelling, and context before opening a link or attachment.

04

Protect your privacy

05

Keep devices updated

LOCK IT DOWN

Password Security Best Practices

Your passwords are the keys to your digital life. Make them strong and keep them safe.

🔑

Use Passphrases

Combine random words to create long passwords that are easy to remember but hard to crack. Example: "BlueTiger$RunsFast2026"

🚫

Never Reuse Passwords

If one account is breached, reused passwords put all your other accounts at risk.

📝

Use a Password Manager

Tools like Bitwarden or Google Password Manager can generate and store strong passwords securely.

🔐

Enable 2FA Everywhere

Two-factor authentication adds a second layer of security beyond just your password.

🔄

Change Compromised Passwords

If you suspect an account is hacked, change the password immediately and check for suspicious activity.

🕵️

Check for Breaches

Visit haveibeenpwned.com to see if your email has appeared in known data breaches.

SOCIAL MEDIA SAFETY

Stay Safe on Social Media

Social media connects us, but it also exposes us. Follow these practices to protect your privacy and reputation.

01

Set Profiles to Private

Only allow people you know and trust to see your posts and personal information.

02

Don't Share Your Location

Avoid posting real-time location updates or tagging your exact address.

03

Be Careful with Photos

Photos can reveal your school, home, or daily routine. Check backgrounds before posting.

04

Don't Accept Strangers

Fake profiles can be used for scams, catfishing, or grooming. Only connect with people you know.

05

Review Tagged Posts

Enable tag review so you can approve or reject posts you're tagged in before they appear on your profile.

06

Log Out on Shared Devices

Always sign out of social media on school computers or borrowed devices.
